Dear IFRC,

I maintain S.K.E.P.I. (Survival Knowledge & Emergency Pocket Intelligence), a free and open-source app
for Android and Windows that works fully offline: an encyclopedia, offline maps and a set of emergency
first aid cards. Source code: https://github.com/Fiouri/skepi (GPL-3.0-or-later).

Following the terms printed in the guidelines ("Copies of all or part of this study may be made for
non-commercial use, providing the source is acknowledged"), I would like to let you know how we use the
International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025:

- The app's first aid cards (CPR for adults, severe bleeding, choking, burns, fractures, hypothermia,
  heatstroke and poisoning) quote short passages from the "First aid steps" and related parts of the
  guidelines word for word. We do not change, summarise or translate the text, and no AI is used to
  write or rewrite it.
- Every card cites the source: the guidelines' title, the 2025 edition, the section and the page. The
  app's About screen carries the IFRC copyright and the licence statement.
- Each card starts with "Call 112 first" (or the user's local emergency number) and the app states that
  it is for general reference and education, is not a medical device, and does not replace first aid
  training or professional care.
- The use is non-commercial: the app is free, has no ads, paid features, analytics or data collection,
  and works without an internet connection. It is distributed through GitHub and, if accepted, F-Droid.

We do not claim any endorsement by the IFRC or by any National Society. If you would prefer a different
form of acknowledgement, would like us to use a different edition, or have any objection to this use,
please tell us and we will follow your guidance.

Thank you for making the guidelines available.
